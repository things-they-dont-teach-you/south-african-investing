# Lesson 7: Spotting investment scams
<!-- facts: Checking a provider and complaining -->

## By the end, the learner can

- List the warning signs of an investment scam.
- Check whether a provider is licensed, by name and FSP number.
- Say where to complain about a fund or an adviser.

## Key ideas

**MTI (Mirror Trading International)** promised up to 10% a month from "bitcoin trading". It
collapsed in 2020, and estimates of what investors lost run into billions of rand. Liquidators
are even claiming back "profits" paid to early investors. No real investment can promise high
returns every month.

**Warning signs:**

- Guaranteed or very high returns, especially "per month".
- Pressure to decide fast, or to bring in friends and family.
- Paying into a personal bank account or a crypto wallet.
- Contact only through WhatsApp, Telegram or social media.
- No FSP number, or one that doesn't match the name.

**Check before you pay.** Anyone who gives financial advice or sells investments must be licensed
by the FSCA as a financial services provider (FSP). Search the FSCA register by name **and**
number (`facts.md`): scammers quote real numbers that belong to other firms.

**If something goes wrong,** complaints are free (`facts.md`). If you think it's a scam, stop
paying and report it to the FSCA.

## Exercise

Make up two offers, clearly labelled as examples: one from a named, licensed fund manager with a
fact sheet, one promising "8% a month, guaranteed" on WhatsApp. Ask the learner which is the
scam, and which warning signs gave it away.
