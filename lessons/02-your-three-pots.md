# Lesson 2: Your retirement fund's three pots
<!-- facts: Two-pot system -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Name the three pots and say what goes into each.
- Say when money can come out of the savings pot, and how it is taxed.
- Explain why a savings-pot withdrawal costs more than the amount it pays out.

## Key ideas

**Who has a retirement fund?** If your payslip shows a pension or provident fund deduction, your
employer runs one for you. A retirement annuity (RA) is one you take out yourself, often used by
the self-employed. Since 1 September 2024 they all follow the two-pot rules (`facts.md`).

**The three pots.**

- The **vested pot** holds what you saved before September 2024, under the old rules.
- The **savings pot** gets one-third of each new contribution. You can take money out once per
  tax year, with a minimum amount (`facts.md`).
- The **retirement pot** gets two-thirds. It stays locked until you retire.

**Withdrawals are taxed at your marginal rate,** the rate on the top slice of your income (the
South African tax course covers this). Fees and any tax you owe SARS come off first. Someone on a
26% rate who withdraws R10 000 gets roughly R7 400, before fees.

**The real cost is the future.** That R10 000 would have kept growing until retirement. Using the
savings pot for emergencies is allowed, but it works best as a last resort, not a yearly bonus.

## Exercise

Ask: Nomsa contributes R3 000 a month to her pension fund. How much goes into each new pot?
(R1 000 to the savings pot, R2 000 to the retirement pot.)
