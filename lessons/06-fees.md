# Lesson 6: Fees, and why they matter so much
<!-- facts: none -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain why a small yearly fee takes a big share over decades.
- Read the TER, TIC and EAC on a fund fact sheet or quote.
- Explain what an index fund is, and why it is usually cheap.

## Key ideas

**A fee is charged on your whole balance, every year,** not just on what you add. Over decades it
compounds against you. Made-up example, at 9% a year growth before fees: R100 000 left for 30
years grows to about R1 156 000 with a 0.5% yearly fee, but only about R661 000 with a 2.5% fee.
Same investment, almost half lost to fees.

**The three numbers to look for:**

- **TER** (total expense ratio): the fund's running costs.
- **TIC** (total investment cost): the TER plus trading costs. It's on the fund fact sheet.
- **EAC** (effective annual cost): adds advice, admin and platform fees too. It is an industry
  standard, and the best number for comparing products. Ask for it before you sign.

Costs bite harder if you leave early: in one industry example, a product cost 1.7% a year over 10
years but 2.6% a year over 3 years.

**Index funds** simply buy every share in a market index, such as the top 40 companies on the
JSE, instead of paying managers to pick. That makes them cheap. Over 10 years to the end of 2025,
S&P found most South African equity funds that pick shares did worse than their index.

## Exercise

Ask the learner what they would ask a salesperson before signing up for an investment.
(At least: the EAC, any advice fee, and whether there are exit fees.)
