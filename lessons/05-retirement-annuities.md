# Lesson 5: Retirement annuities and the tax deduction
<!-- facts: Retirement fund contributions; Two-pot system -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain what a retirement annuity (RA) is and who it suits.
- Say how contributions reduce income tax, and the limits.
- Compare an RA with a TFSA.

## Key ideas

**An RA is a retirement fund you take out yourself.** It suits the self-employed and anyone whose
employer has no fund. It follows the same two-pot rules as an employer fund (lesson 2).

**The tax deduction.** Contributions to all your retirement funds together are deductible up to a
percentage of income and a yearly cap (`facts.md`). The saving is your marginal rate: someone on
a 31% rate who puts R1 000 into an RA pays about R310 less tax. Employees usually get this
through their payslip or their tax return; the South African tax course explains how.

**RA or TFSA?**

| | RA | TFSA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax break | When you put money in | When you take money out |
| Access | Locked until retirement, apart from the savings pot | Any time |
| Best for | Saving for retirement | Long-term goals you might need before retirement |

Many people use both: an RA for the tax deduction, a TFSA for flexibility.

## Exercise

Ask: Zinhle earns R25 000 a month and her top tax rate is 26%. If she puts R1 500 a month into an
RA, roughly how much tax does that save in a year? (R18 000 × 26% ≈ R4 680.)
