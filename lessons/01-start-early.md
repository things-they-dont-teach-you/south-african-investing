# Lesson 1: Why starting early matters
<!-- facts: none -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain compound growth in their own words.
- Say why starting ten years earlier can beat saving for thirty years longer.
- Explain how inflation shrinks money left in cash.

## Key ideas

**Compound growth** means your money earns growth, and then that growth earns growth too. The
longer it runs, the faster it snowballs. Time does most of the work, not the amount.

**Starting early.** These examples use a made-up 7% a year growth rate, to show the idea; real
returns go up and down and are never guaranteed.

- Thandi saves R500 a month from age 25 to 35, then stops and leaves it to grow. She puts in
  R60 000. At 65 she has about R702 000.
- Lebo starts at 35 and saves R500 a month until 65. He puts in R180 000, three times as much.
  At 65 he has about R610 000.

Thandi's ten extra years of growth beat Lebo's twenty extra years of saving.

**Inflation** means prices rise, so the same rand buys less each year. If prices rise 5% a year,
R10 000 kept under the mattress for ten years buys only about what R6 100 buys today. Money only
really grows if it grows faster than inflation.

**Emergency money first.** Before investing for the long term, keep a small cushion in an
account you can reach quickly, so a broken fridge doesn't force you to cash in your retirement
savings (lesson 3 shows what that costs).

## Exercise

Ask: if the learner could afford R200 a month, would they rather start now, or wait five years
and save R300 a month? Talk through why, without calculating exact figures.
