# Lesson 8: Retiring: lump sums, annuities and the state grant
<!-- facts: Retiring from a fund; Retirement lump-sum tax table; Living annuity drawdown; SASSA Older Person's Grant -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Say how much of their fund they can take in cash at retirement.
- Work out the tax on a retirement lump sum.
- Explain the difference between a living annuity and a guaranteed (life) annuity.
- Say what the state Older Person's Grant pays, and who qualifies.

## Key ideas

**At retirement** you can take part of your vested pot in cash; the rest, and the retirement pot,
must buy an annuity, a monthly pension. Small funds can be taken entirely in cash (`facts.md`).

**Lump-sum tax.** The retirement table is far kinder than the withdrawal table: a big first slice
is tax-free (`facts.md`). Example: a first ever lump sum of R1 000 000 is taxed R39 600 + 27% of
R230 000 = **R101 700**. Earlier withdrawals shrink the tax-free slice (lesson 3).

**Two kinds of annuity:**

- A **guaranteed (life) annuity** pays an income for as long as you live, however long that is.
  The insurer carries the risk.
- A **living annuity** stays invested in your name. You choose an income within a range each year
  (`facts.md`). What's left goes to your family, but if you draw too much, or markets fall, it can
  run out while you're still alive.

The choice is hard to undo; this is a good moment to see a licensed financial adviser.

**The state grant** (`facts.md`) is a safety net from age 60, means-tested on income and assets.
It is not enough to live on comfortably, which is why lessons 1 to 5 matter.
