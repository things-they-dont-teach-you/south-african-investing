# Lesson 3: Changing jobs: preserve, don't cash out
<!-- facts: Two-pot system; Withdrawal lump-sum tax table; Retirement lump-sum tax table -->

## By the end, the learner can

- List their choices for their retirement money when they leave a job.
- Work out the tax on cashing out a vested pot, using the withdrawal table.
- Explain how cashing out now raises the tax they pay at retirement.

## Key ideas

**When you leave a job,** your savings pot and retirement pot move with you. The vested pot (your
pre-2024 savings) can still be taken in cash under the old rules. You can instead keep it
invested: move it to your new employer's fund, or into a preservation fund or retirement
annuity.

**Cashing out is taxed on a different table** from your salary (`facts.md`). Only a small first
slice is tax-free, and the table is cumulative: SARS adds up every withdrawal you take in your
life.

Example: Sipho resigns and cashes out a R100 000 vested pot, his first ever withdrawal. The
first R27 500 is tax-free, and 18% of the remaining R72 500 is **R13 050** in tax.

**It also costs him at retirement.** The retirement table has a much bigger tax-free amount, but
it counts earlier withdrawals. Sipho has already used R100 000 of it.

**And he loses the growth.** Lesson 1 showed what money left alone for decades can become.

Before deciding, ask the fund what each option costs. If the learner urgently needs cash, talk
about the savings pot (lesson 2) and other options before the vested pot.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to work out the tax on a first withdrawal of R50 000 using the table in
