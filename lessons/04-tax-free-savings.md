# Lesson 4: Tax-free savings accounts
<!-- facts: Tax-free savings accounts -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain what a tax-free savings account (TFSA) is and what it can hold.
- State the annual and lifetime limits, and what happens if they go over.
- Explain why a TFSA suits long-term saving better than an emergency fund.

## Key ideas

**A TFSA is a wrapper,** not an investment. Banks offer cash TFSAs; investment companies offer
TFSAs that hold unit trusts or exchange-traded funds (ETFs). Inside it, you pay no tax on
interest, dividends or growth.

**Limits** (`facts.md`): there is a yearly limit and a lifetime limit, counted across all your
TFSAs at every provider together. Going over costs a penalty tax, so keep your own record if
you have more than one.

**Withdrawals don't give you the limit back.** If you put in the full yearly limit, take some out
and put it back, the putting-back counts again. That is why a TFSA works best for money you plan
to leave alone for years. Keep emergency money in an ordinary account.

**Unlike a retirement fund,** you can take TFSA money out whenever you want, and there is no tax
deduction when you put it in. Lesson 5 compares the two.

## Exercise

Ask: Aisha puts R40 000 into a TFSA at one bank and R10 000 at another in the same tax year.
What's the problem? (Together she is over the yearly limit, so the excess is penalised.)
